## Goal

CI checks that PyTorch and JAX give the same gradients up to numerical precision, and comparable syntheses, so that a result does not depend on the backend.

## Brief

**Question 1. How are syntheses compared across backends?** PyTorch's `LBFGS` with strong Wolfe (M-SYN-LBFGS) and `optax.lbfgs` with a zoom line search are different implementations; §17.3 expects different trajectories.
(a) Trajectories matched over the first iterations, from the same start in float64, with both optimisers set alike (history, line-search constants, first step): only if they can be made to agree.
(b) One L-BFGS written on the L0 protocol for both backends: agreement by construction, but it replaces two maintained optimisers (§17.3; Part II §12.3) for the sake of a test.
(c) Statistical criteria only (§17.3): the final losses, and the statistics of the syntheses of each backend within a tolerance of each other, over a few seeds.
**Recommendation:** status `study`: try (a) on the planar synthesis of T6.1; keep (a) with (c) if the losses agree over a few iterations, else (c) alone.

## Inputs

Part III §17.3; Part I §3.2.2, §5.2 (the row "PyTorch vs JAX"); Part II §12.3 (`lbfgs`), §15.2, T6.1 (its comparison over the first iterations); maths M-SYN-LBFGS; Part IV §22, S13; the cards LF1, W2.
Modules: `tests/differentiation/`, `tests/_backends.py`, a cross-backend test module; `src/estel/backend/torch/_optim.py`, `src/estel/backend/jax/_optim.py` (LF1).

## Tasks

1. Gradients across backends: the gradient of a loss of the statistics and of the spectra, PyTorch against JAX, in float64 and float32, on small cases of every geometry (needs only the experimental backend).
2. The study of question 1 (a), once LF1 provides the optimiser: the iterations over which the losses agree, reported on the issue.
3. [human] Choose (a) with (c), or (c) alone, from the study.
4. The synthesis comparison in the full tier, with the references per backend of W2.

## Done when

A test compares the gradients of every geometry across backends within the row "PyTorch vs JAX" of `tests/tolerances.toml`; a full-tier test compares the syntheses of both backends by the chosen criterion; each test runs in under 1 s or is marked slow with its reason.

## Feature

Cross-backend checks: syntheses matched over a few iterations when the optimisers can be standardised; gradients backend-independent up to numerical precision
