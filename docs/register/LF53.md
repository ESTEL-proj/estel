## Goal

In every geometry, `to_vector` keeps the imaginary parts of the cross-channel S1 and S2, the phase between two channels, so that `to_vector` and the synthesis loss see the same coefficients (OP-39); the cross-spectra, real for real data, are documented as such.

## Brief

**Question 1. What of the cross-spectra (OP-19)?** For real data a radially binned cross-spectrum is real: the `fft` estimator sums each mode with its conjugate (rFFT modes counted twice), and the band maps of the three other estimators are real.
- (a) nothing more to keep: OP-19 closed as documentation, no change of values.
- (b) a complex, oriented cross-spectrum (a sum over a half-plane, whose imaginary part carries a shift between the channels): a new statistic, not invariant under a rotation by π.
- **Recommendation:** (a), because the imaginary part that OP-19 drops is zero; a spectrum of the phase between channels would be a new feature, registered as such if wanted.

**Question 2. How are the cross S1 and S2 flattened to a real vector (OP-37; the definition to write)?**
- (a) the rule of LF20 (its question 2): both parts of every complex entry, one real for an entry real by construction (auto S1, S2 and PS); `to_vector` grows by the number of cross S1 and S2 entries.
- (b) both parts of every S1 and S2 entry, auto terms included: zeros in the vector.
- **Recommendation:** (a), which LF20 then already applies; this entry makes the synthesis loss read `to_vector`, whose value does not change: the loss of 1.0.0 is already the complete one (M-SYN-LOSS).
