## Goal

In every geometry, `to_vector` keeps the imaginary parts of the cross-channel S1 and S2, the phase between two channels, so that `to_vector` and the synthesis loss see the same coefficients (OP-39). The cross-spectra are real once ring-averaged, and are documented as such (OP-19).

## Decisions

- 20 November 2026, Pablo Richard: ring-averaged cross-spectra of real fields are real, and ring averages are what ESTEL computes. The `fft` estimator counts each rFFT mode twice, so its real part is the full-plane value; the band maps of the other estimators are real. OP-19 drops nothing: it is closed as documentation, with no change of values. A spectrum of the directional phase between channels would be a new feature (`docs/register/decisions.md`; PR #118).
- 24 November 2026, Pablo Richard: the cross S1 and S2 are flattened by the rule of LF20, its question 2 (question 1, answer (a)): both parts of every complex entry, one real for an entry real by construction (auto S1, S2 and PS); `to_vector` grows by the number of cross S1 and S2 entries, and the synthesis loss reads it (OP-37, OP-39) (`docs/register/decisions.md`).

## Feature

Complex cross-channel terms kept: the imaginary part of the cross-channel S1 and S2 when flattened to a real vector (OP-37). The cross-spectra need nothing: ring-averaged, they are real (OP-19, closed on 20 November 2026)

## Why

the imaginary part of the cross-channel S1 and S2 carries the phase between two channels (a shift of one against the other) and is dropped with no stated reason; with it, `to_vector` and the synthesis loss see the same coefficients (OP-39)
