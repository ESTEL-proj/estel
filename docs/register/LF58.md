## Goal

A synthesis gives the same trajectory and the same result whether its target is given as maps or as the statistics of those maps: one meaning of `mean_field`, one standardisation, one loss and one form of the result; for every geometry with syntheses (periodic 2D data in 1.0.0, T6.1).

## Decisions

- 25 November 2026, Pablo Richard: the loss is not divided, from statistics as from maps (question 2, answer (a)). In 1.0.0 a synthesis from statistics with `mean_field=True` divides it by the target's Nb, which rescales the gradient, hence the first step of L-BFGS and what its tolerances mean (OP-71). The choice is documented in the code, in the docstrings of `synthesize_from_maps` and `synthesize_from_stats`, and in the documentation (`docs/register/decisions.md`).
- 25 November 2026, Pablo Richard, with question 2: the logs of a synthesis show, beside the loss, an intensive loss: the loss divided by the number of real terms it sums, so that syntheses with different configurations and losses compare. The documentation gives typical ranges of the intensive loss, for example "above 10^a: likely not converged; below 10^b: likely fitting the target's own sample noise", measured on the synthesis tests of W2. A reference level to measure with them is the intensive loss between the statistics of two independent draws of the target's process: the sample noise of the statistics (`docs/register/decisions.md`).
- 25 November 2026, Pablo Richard: one concept for `mean_field`, with the pooled standardisation of synthesis from maps (M-SYN-TARGET) everywhere (question 1, answer (b), not the recommendation (a)): one μ_c and one σ_c per channel, σ_c² = (1/Nb) Σ_b ⟨(x_b,c − μ_c)²⟩. The statistics API gains a batch standardisation (an API addition, a minor, through `api/manifest.json` first). A synthesis from statistics computed without it, with `mean_field=True`, raises a warning, not an error: the warning says to recompute the statistics with the batch standardisation, and the synthesis then runs on the statistics as they are. The result is de-standardised with those pooled μ_c and σ_c. Synthesis from maps is then the statistics of the pre-filtered maps, with the batch standardisation, then synthesis from statistics: one code path (revised the same day: below). Chosen for the mean-field approach, consistently with LF59's question 2, answer (b): the target ensemble keeps its fluctuations of amplitude (`docs/register/decisions.md`).
- 25 November 2026, Pablo Richard: the result follows the layout of the target, with `nbatch` in place of the batch axis (question 3, answer (b), widened to both axes). A 2D target (N₁, N₂) gives (N₁, N₂) for `nbatch` = 1 and (nbatch, N₁, N₂) otherwise; a 3D target (Nc, N₁, N₂) gives (Nc, N₁, N₂) or (nbatch, Nc, N₁, N₂); a 4D target (Nb, Nc, N₁, N₂) always gives (nbatch, Nc, N₁, N₂). HEALPix is the same with one pixel axis. The statistics record the layout of the input they were computed from (one static field), so that synthesis from statistics follows the same rule. It replaces the rule of 1.0.0 (M-SYN-OUT, OP-75) (`docs/register/decisions.md`).
- 25 November 2026, Pablo Richard, revising the decision above on question 1: the target is no longer pre-filtered to the Nyquist disk before its statistics (LF59). Synthesis from maps is therefore the statistics of the maps, not pre-filtered, with the batch standardisation, then synthesis from statistics: still one code path. The pooled μ_c and σ_c are computed on the target as given. The Nyquist filter acts on the running field only (LF59) (`docs/register/decisions.md`).

## Feature

One loss function for both entries of the syntheses, from maps and from statistics, whatever the path followed: one normalisation of the loss, one standardisation and one form of the result

## Why

the loss is divided by Nb from statistics only (OP-71), the two entries standardise differently (OP-72), so one target gives two trajectories, and they remove the channel axis of the result under different rules (OP-75)
