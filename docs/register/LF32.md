## Goal

`iso` and `angular_fourier` consistent with the conjugation of the orientations across π, in planar 2D and on HEALPix (whose gauges also cover [0, π)), defined in 1D whatever L (OP-40), the low-pass terms of LF6 included; LF26 extends them to tensor products.

## Brief

**Question 1. Does `iso` return only the real parts of the reduced S3 and S4, and of the cross S1 and S2, which have the same defect (maths_step2 M-ISO2)?**
- (a) yes: the real part is the average over the 2L band orientations of [0, 2π), invariant under rotations by π/L; the reduced S3 and S4 halve.
- (b) no, complex as in STL: the imaginary parts, about a tenth of the real parts, depend on which half-plane is called [0, π), and vanish in expectation for an isotropic field.
- **Recommendation:** (a), because nothing invariant is lost, and `iso` becomes a linear map on the real parts of the canonical set (LF20), with no conjugation.

**Question 2. What does `iso` mean in 1D with L = 2, where ℓ = 1 is the conjugate filter (OP-40)?**
- (a) the average over the two directions of the line: the real parts, for L = 1 and L = 2 alike; the time asymmetry that the imaginary parts carry is lost, and L = 1 changes too.
- (b) the line has no rotation: `iso` gives the statistics of L = 1, the entries of ℓ = 1 being conjugates or copies of those of ℓ = 0 on real data (LF20 drops them); nothing is lost, and L = 1 is unchanged.
- (c) a clear error with L = 2.
- **Recommendation:** (b), because `iso` averages over rotations; the same rule leaves the 1D blocks of tensor products alone (LF26).

**Question 3. Without `iso`, `angular_fourier` transforms the band orientation, which comes back conjugated after π instead of periodic (M-RED-ANG): what then?**
- (a) `iso` required first, as for `scale_cosine`: every orientation axis left is π-periodic and the transform exact; an error where 1.0.0 returns a value.
- (b) a transform over the 2L orientations of [0, 2π), extended by conjugation: exact, with twice the harmonics, half of them redundant.
- **Recommendation:** (a), because it keeps one exact definition; an orientation-resolved transform can be registered as a feature if a need appears.
