## Decisions

- 24 November 2026, Pablo Richard: exactly one block of a product carries one sign of frequency, and every other block carries both: a 2D block with its orientations over [0, 2π) (2L orientations), a 1D block with L = 2. One 2D block with 1D blocks is STL's setup, unchanged; 2D+2D needs one 2D block over [0, 2π), 2D+2D+2D two of them. The cost falls only on these products (for 2D+2D, the first layer doubles and the S4 orientations grow by up to ×8). Which block carries the single sign, a convention, is still to decide (`docs/register/decisions.md`).
- 24 November 2026, Pablo Richard: products of 1D blocks only drop their duplicate half (one 1D block with L = 1): confirmed, since on a real field conj ψ₀ ⊗ conj ψ₁ gives the conjugate response of ψ₀ ⊗ ψ₁, with the same arms; this follows from LF20's question 1 (`docs/register/decisions.md`).

## Feature

The product orientations of a tensor product cover every distinct product filter of a real field, whatever its blocks: with two or more 2D blocks (2D+2D, 2D+2D+1D), both ψ_a ⊗ ψ_b and ψ_a ⊗ conj ψ_b, for example by giving one block its orientations over [0, 2π); with 1D blocks only, the half that duplicates the other by conjugation computed once

## Why

the argument "Why L = 2 in 1D blocks" of M-W1-TP holds with exactly one 2D block: a 1D block with L = 2 then supplies both sign combinations. With two or more 2D blocks, half of the distinct product filters are never computed; with 1D blocks only, half of them are computed twice. Step 1 reproduces STL's orientations as they are
