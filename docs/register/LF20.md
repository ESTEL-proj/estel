## Goal

One canonical set of coefficients in every geometry (planar 2D and 1D, tensor products, HEALPix), the low-pass terms of LF6 included: what is computed, stored, returned by `to_vector` and weighted in the synthesis loss, while `full_grid` still gives the tensors of 1.0.0.

## Brief

**Question 1. Which set is canonical (Part III, §19.8; maths_step2 M-CANON)?**
- (a) STL's set, packing being a storage choice only: the duplicates count twice in `to_vector` and in the loss, so the values and losses of 1.0.0 are kept.
- (b) the non-redundant set: at j₁ = j₂, S4 keeps ℓ₁ ≤ ℓ₂ (auto) and one channel order (cross); 1D with L = 2 and products of 1D blocks only keep one of two conjugate orientations; S4 is 11 to 19 % smaller for an auto term and 3/(2(J + 2)), 15 to 25 %, for a cross pair (J = 4 to 8); the losses change.
- **Recommendation:** (b), because a duplicate carries no information, its double weight in the loss is an accident of storage, and step 2 keeps no behaviour of STL.

**Question 2. What does `to_vector` return, and how does an entry real by construction count (M-CANON, "Open")?**
- (a) the canonical set: one real for an entry real by construction (auto S1, S2 and PS; the diagonal of S4, c₁ = c₂, j₁ = j₂, ℓ₁ = ℓ₂; every term whose last filter is low-pass in every block, Φ being real), two for any other, as the `is_complex` of a segment (Part III, §19.4).
- (b) the canonical set, two reals for every entry: zeros in the vector.
- (c) the full grid, duplicates included, as in 1.0.0.
- **Recommendation:** (a), because its squared norm is then the complex loss of M-SYN-LOSS on the canonical set: `to_vector` and the loss become one vector (OP-39, with LF53).
