## Goal

Every change of step 2 is judged where users run ESTEL: public cases for every geometry at a small and a medium size, a synthesis case and a larger case, measured on PyTorch and JAX on a pinned GPU node (tier 2) against thresholds chosen by humans; then a baseline and an optimisation session on native x86 Linux and on GPU settle the choices that depend on the platform (Part III, §19.14).

## Brief

**Question 1. The datasets of the cases.** Step 1's cases are seeded fields, the exponential of a Gaussian field with a power-law spectrum (`bench/_cases.py`, as in `tests/consistency/`); nothing in the repository downloads data. Part I §5.1 points at two public sources without naming a dataset: the data of STL's certification notebooks and the advected turbulence case of the STOC deliverable.
- (a) Seeded fields only: no download, no licence, any size from a seed; time and memory do not depend on the values (shapes and masks are static), but the iterations of a synthesis depend on its target.
- (b) Public datasets for every case: content users recognise; a licence, a checksum and a stored copy per dataset, staged on the node; sizes set by cropping the data.
- (c) Seeded fields for every timed case, a public target for the synthesis case: no threshold depends on a download; real content enters only where it changes a result.
- **Recommendation:** (c), because the cost of ESTEL does not depend on the values, while question 4 and LF59 count iterations, which depend on the target. The datasets are chosen with the maintainers (Part I §6.3), from the two sources of §5.1.

**Question 2. The thresholds of `bench/thresholds.toml`** (protected; the file does not exist yet). Step 1 has none: a change is judged by the median ratio to `main` over alternating rounds, against the noise floor of `main` against `main` (±2.5 % on the laptop, Part I §6.3). The file is to hold the significant change of each metric, a factor 2 in speed or peak memory to begin with, which earns a minor (`docs/policy/VERSIONING.md`), and JAX's compile-time budgets (LF1).
- (a) Ratios to `main` per case and metric, both measured on the node in one session (step 1's comparison mode): the drift of the node cancels; each run measures two commits.
- (b) Ratios to a stored baseline: one commit per run; a new driver, a new pinned version or the load of a shared node reads as a change, so the baseline is measured again after each.
- (c) Absolute values per case (seconds, GiB on the device): easy to read, valid on one node only; the natural form of a budget.
- (d) Against STL: its container is pinned and CPU only (PyTorch 2.15.0+cpu), so it judges nothing on GPU; a record in the baseline, not a threshold.
- **Recommendation:** (a), with (c) for the compile-time budgets. A gain counts from a factor 2 (Part I §6.3); a regression beyond twice the range of `main` against `main` over 5 rounds, measured on the node for each case and recorded in the file: the laptop's ±2.5 % says nothing of the node, and Part I §6.2 finds a shared HPC node too noisy to gate a merge. The allocated peak of the device counts the arrays exactly (LF36), so memory takes a smaller margin than time.

**Question 3. TF32 on NVIDIA GPUs of the Ampere generation and later.** With the pinned versions, PyTorch 2.15.0 runs cuDNN convolutions in TF32 (`torch.backends.cudnn.allow_tf32` is True by default) and matrix products in float32 (`torch.backends.cuda.matmul.allow_tf32` is False). On JAX 0.10.2, `conv_axes` asks for `Precision.HIGHEST`, but the Gram product (`xp.matmul`, `src/estel/data/_moments.py`) takes the default precision, TF32 on A100 and H100 by JAX's documentation. Nothing in `src/estel/` sets either. TF32 rounds each input to a 10-bit mantissa (unit roundoff 2⁻¹¹ ≈ 4.9e-4); Part I §5.2 plans float32 tolerances of 1e-4 against the references and 1e-5 between PyTorch and JAX.
- (a) Disabled during ESTEL's computations, in L0 on both backends, the user's settings restored on return: float32 keeps its precision on GPU; the speed of TF32 is lost; on PyTorch, a backward pass the user runs after ESTEL returns may read the user's setting [to verify on the node].
- (b) Kept as the libraries set it, if the tolerances hold: the fastest; the two backends round different operations, so on one GPU they would differ by TF32's error [not measured], and the GPU rows of `tests/tolerances.toml` are calibrated on it (T8.1).
- (c) Disabled by default, with a public opt-in to TF32: a new capability, so a card of its own and an entry in `api/manifest.json` (golden rules 7 and 11).
- **Recommendation:** (a), because statistics never change silently (golden rule 1), TF32's rounding is 5 to 50 times the planned float32 tolerances, and JAX's convolutions already run at full precision. The session measures the time with and without TF32 on each GPU case; a large gain is the case for (c).

**Question 4. The default length of the L-BFGS history** (`history_size`: 50 pairs, STL's, M-SYN-LBFGS). T6.1 measured it (#28, decision 1 of 21 November 2026): at 64 × 64 and 128 × 128, J = 4 and 5, float64, 50 iterations, three seeds, 10 and 20 pairs end within 0 to 6 % of the loss of 50 pairs, below the spread of the seeds. The time per iteration does not depend on it; the memory does, two numbers per pixel and pair.
- (a) 50: nothing moves; 100 MiB per 512 × 512 map in float32, about an eighth of the 0.81 GiB that the scattering with its gradient takes there (J = 7, PyTorch, CPU).
- (b) 20: 60 MiB less per 512 × 512 map; trajectories move (`SYNTHESIS_REFERENCE` bumped if W2's references exist), and a checkpoint written with 50 resumes only with `history_size=50`, as its error says.
- (c) 10: 80 MiB less per 512 × 512 map; the same consequences as (b).
- **Recommendation:** decide after the session (task 7): the smaller of (c) and (b) whose final loss at `max_iter` = 100 stays within the spread of the seeds of that of 50 pairs, on the synthesis case and the larger case, if it lowers the device peak beyond the noise; else (a). Only memory is at stake, and T6.1 measured small fields over half the default iterations.

## Tasks

Phase 2a.0 builds the benchmark (tasks 1 to 5), which LF6, LF7, LF20, LF33, LF52 and LF59 use; phase 2a.5 holds the baseline and the session (tasks 6 to 8), with LF36's measurement.
1. [human] Questions 1 to 3 answered on the issue and logged in `docs/register/decisions.md`; question 4 after task 7.
2. The cases, added to `bench/cases.toml`: each geometry at a small and a medium size, the 2D+1D cases of the STOC deliverable at reduced sizes, HEALPix once it exists, the synthesis case with its time per iteration, the larger case (a larger map or a batch of maps); PyTorch and JAX, float32; every run checks its values (Part I §6.3).
3. The harness on GPU: the allocated peak of the device and JAX's compile time; CUDA builds of torch 2.15.0 and jax 0.10.2, which neither `uv.lock` nor the parity container installs (both take PyTorch 2.15.0+cpu on Linux), recorded with each result.
4. Tier 2 (Part I §6.1): the pinned GPU node, the `bench` label, results committed by a bot with a hardware fingerprint; the noise floor of each case measured there [human: the node and the bot's access].
5. `bench/thresholds.toml` per question 2 [human, protected]; TF32 per question 3, in L0, with CPU tests that a call leaves the user's settings as it found them.
6. The baseline on the node, committed in `bench/results/`; STL's times on tensor products, once, in its container (Part I §6.3).
7. The session: each candidate of the section Feature judged per question 2, on x86 and GPU, LF36 included; the history at 10, 20 and 50 pairs on the synthesis case and the larger case (three seeds, `max_iter` = 100: device peak, time per iteration, final loss); the time with and without TF32; results in `bench/results/` and on the issue, then in Part III §19.14 [human: what is kept].
8. If question 4 changes the default: `history_size` in `synthesize_from_maps`, `synthesize_from_stats` and `lbfgs`; M-SYN-LBFGS [human]; `SYNTHESIS_REFERENCE` bumped if W2's references exist [human].

## Done when

- `bench/cases.toml` holds the cases of task 2 for every geometry ESTEL has; on the node, each records its time, its device peak and, on JAX, its compile time, and checks its values.
- Tier 2 runs on the `bench` label and commits its results with a hardware fingerprint; `bench/thresholds.toml` is approved by the CODEOWNERS, with the noise floor of each case and LF1's compile-time budgets.
- With question 3 applied, float32 statistics and gradients on GPU equal those on CPU within the float32 tolerance of `tests/tolerances.toml`, on both backends; an ESTEL call leaves the user's TF32 settings unchanged.
- The baseline is in `bench/results/`; each candidate of the session is kept or dropped in Part III §19.14, LF36's measurement included.
- Question 4 is decided from task 7 and logged in `docs/register/decisions.md`.

## Feature

The benchmark of step 2: public cases for every geometry, at a small and a medium size (tensor products, the 2D+1D cases of the STOC deliverable at reduced sizes, HEALPix, a synthesis with its time per iteration), their datasets, the baseline, the thresholds of `bench/thresholds.toml` and tier 2 (Part I, §6.1, §6.3); an optimisation session on native x86 Linux and on GPU, which confirms the conclusions of step 1 measured on CPU and decides the choices that depend on the platform, with a larger case (a larger map or a batch of maps) and the memory on the device. Candidates measured there: `torch.compile` of the scattering computation on PyTorch, whose counterpart on JAX divided the times by 2 to 15 (T2.13); the per-scale convolutions (issue #91); level-wise gradients on GPU and at larger sizes (LF36, issue #92); the memory of JAX's compiled loop, which on CPU uses more than PyTorch (about 1.0 against 0.8 GiB, and 1.1 against 0.5 GiB on planar-cross); from T3.5 (issue #128): one call for the complex intermediate maps of tensor products, on JAX through an L0 primitive per backend, with the assembly of complex values from real pairs; `conv3d` for the planar 2D convolution of large batches; the order of the downsampling in tensor products, the real field brought to its level before the filtering (M-W1-TP)

the benchmark of step 1 is light: 2D cases on CPU, which check that ESTEL's time and memory are commensurate with STL's and judge the optimisations of step 1
