## Feature

The benchmark of step 2: public cases for every geometry, at a small and a medium size (tensor products, the 2D+1D cases of the STOC deliverable at reduced sizes, HEALPix, a synthesis with its time per iteration), their datasets, the baseline, the thresholds of `bench/thresholds.toml` and tier 2 (Part I, §6.1, §6.3); an optimisation session on native x86 Linux and on GPU, which confirms the conclusions of step 1 measured on CPU and decides the choices that depend on the platform, with a larger case (a larger map or a batch of maps) and the memory on the device. Candidates measured there: `torch.compile` of the scattering computation on PyTorch, whose counterpart on JAX divided the times by 2 to 15 (T2.13); the per-scale convolutions (issue #91); level-wise gradients on GPU and at larger sizes (LF36, issue #92); the memory of JAX's compiled loop, which on CPU uses more than PyTorch (about 1.0 against 0.8 GiB, and 1.1 against 0.5 GiB on planar-cross)

## Why

the benchmark of step 1 is light: 2D cases on CPU, which check that ESTEL's time and memory are commensurate with STL's and judge the optimisations of step 1

## Builds on (reserved in 1.0.0)

`bench/` and its comparison mode (T2.11)

## To decide

the public datasets of the cases; the thresholds; TF32 on NVIDIA GPUs of the Ampere generation and later, where PyTorch computes convolutions in TF32 by default (about 3 significant digits instead of 7 for float32) without warning: disabled during ESTEL's computations, or kept for speed if the parity tolerance holds; the default length of the L-BFGS history, refined after T6.1
