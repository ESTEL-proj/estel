## Feature

The HEALPix tables of an operator read back from disk instead of being built again in each new process

## Why

measured on 25 November 2026 (T5.7, Apple M3, full sky, L = 4, K = 5, a mask of 10 %, every table of the operator over all its levels, an uncompressed `.npz` read in a fresh process from a warm page cache): at `nside` 64 (J = 5), 283 MiB in float64, a build of 6.0 s against a read of 0.06 s; at `nside` 128 (J = 6), 1.11 GiB, a build of 25.4 s against a read of 0.23 s. A cold read from disk is not measured. Within one process a cache saves nothing, since one operator serves every normalisation

## Builds on (reserved in 1.0.0)

the tables built in NumPy with the plan (T5.1, T5.7)

## To decide

where the cache lives (an option of `STOperator`, an environment variable or a default directory); its key and its invalidation (depth, `cell_ids`, L, K, the mask, the version of the tables); on or off by default; the dtype stored

## Details

STL's healpix-analyse has such a cache (`conv_cache_dir`), not kept in 1.0.0 (Part II, §10): it stores the weights in float32, which changes S3 and S4 (maths OP-46). Part III, §19.14 (trial 5 of T5.7). Suggested step: 2b, with the Rust engine (LF3, LF5), which has its own tables, or 2a.X
