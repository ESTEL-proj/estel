## Goal

`iso` on tensor-product statistics (space–time 2D+1D, 2D+1D+1D), where 1.0.0 raises a clear error (OP-44): an isotropy in space that keeps the direction of propagation. `angular_fourier` and `scale_cosine` on tensor products moved to LF68 (step 3); in step 2 they keep the error of 1.0.0.

## Decisions

- 25 November 2026, Pablo Richard: `iso` on products is one parameter (questions 1 and 2): `STStatistics.reduce(iso=...)` takes `False`, `True`, or a sequence of block indices (their positions in `J` and `L`). `True` averages over the rotations of every 2D block, over [0, 2π), and over nothing on the 1D blocks: planar 2D gives LF32's real parts, pure 1D the statistics of L = 1, one 2D block with 1D blocks keeps the direction of propagation (question 1, answer (a)), and several 2D blocks average every 2D block, each rotated independently (`docs/register/decisions.md`).
- 25 November 2026, Pablo Richard: a sequence averages over exactly those blocks: a 2D block over its rotations, a 1D block over time reversal (question 1, answer (b), is `iso=(0, 1)` on 2D+1D), with a UserWarning when a 1D block is included: "averaging block a over time reversal drops the direction of propagation; its imaginary parts are lost." Several 2D blocks raise no error: every subset is allowed, which LF63's covering makes computable. A joint rotation (the same angle in several 2D blocks) is not offered. Widening `iso` from `bool` to `bool | Sequence[int]` is an API addition (a minor), through `api/manifest.json` first (`docs/register/decisions.md`).
- 25 November 2026, Pablo Richard: question 3 is postponed to step 3, as the new entry LF68: in step 2, `angular_fourier` and `scale_cosine` on tensor-product statistics keep the clear error of 1.0.0 (OP-44). Block by block rests on the componentwise order of product scales, which LF27 (step 3) questions; deciding both together avoids defining `scale_cosine` twice (`docs/register/decisions.md`).
