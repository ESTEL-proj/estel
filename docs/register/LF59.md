## Goal

A synthesis starts from a Gaussian field with the target's spectrum inside the Nyquist disk, instead of a white noise of variance near π/4 (0.78 at 32 × 32); `var`, the only statistic of the loss that sees beyond the disk, leaves the loss, and the power beyond the disk stays zero; for every geometry with syntheses (periodic planar data in 1.0.0, T6.1).

## Decisions

- 25 November 2026, Pablo Richard: the start is scaled over the running batch (question 2, answer (b), not the recommendation (a)): the batch of starts is scaled together, not each start, chosen for the mean-field approach; the starts keep their sample fluctuations about the batch (`docs/register/decisions.md`).
- 25 November 2026, Pablo Richard: `var` leaves the synthesis loss; it stays in the statistics for users. It was the only statistic of the loss that sees beyond the Nyquist disk. Inside the disk, the power is constrained by S2, in every wavelet band, and by the spectrum, and the zero mode by `mean`; the lowest frequencies, below the coarsest wavelet, are covered by the low-pass term S2[J] of LF6, which planar 2D does not have in 1.0.0: LF59 depends on LF6. The standardisation of LF58 (the pooled μ and σ, computed with the statistics of the pre-filtered maps, as in M-SYN-TARGET; whether they include the scales beyond the disk does not matter for a convenience scale) is now a convenience scale, for values of order one, not a constraint (`docs/register/decisions.md`).
- 25 November 2026, Pablo Richard: the power beyond the disk is then neither constrained nor moved by the loss. The start is band-limited, and the running field is projected on the disk at every evaluation: the loss is computed on P(v), with P the pre-filter (a projection), at the cost of one FFT pair per evaluation, so the power beyond the disk stays exactly zero. Statistics outside the loss, the PDF for instance, are compared with the pre-filtered target. How much power the gradients of 1.0.0 leak beyond the disk, through the 5 × 5 wavelets and the smoothing kernel cropped in pixel space, is to be measured (task 3) (`docs/register/decisions.md`).
- 25 November 2026, Pablo Richard: the start is the spectral start (question 1): a Gaussian field with the target's spectrum inside the disk, drawn from the binned spectrum, the smooth curve the loss constrains, not from the target's periodogram with random phases, which would copy its sample noise. It is the maximum-entropy field under second-order constraints. When the target's statistics hold no spectrum, the start is band-limited white noise scaled so that its variance inside the disk equals the target's, from S2 with LF6's low-pass term. The batch of starts is scaled together (question 2) (`docs/register/decisions.md`).
- 25 November 2026, Pablo Richard: the Nyquist filter, for the start and for the projection, may also be coded as a kernel filtering option, a convolution in pixel space, besides the cut in Fourier space; the M- entry states both. A kernel is not an exact projection: with it, "no power beyond the disk" holds only approximately (`docs/register/decisions.md`).

## The Nyquist disk

Pablo Richard's rationale for this card, documented here at his request on 25 November 2026 (`docs/register/decisions.md`).

Except in 1D, `var` is the only statistic of the synthesis loss that sees every scale. The wavelets do not cover the corners of the frequency square (OP-32), the radial bins of the spectrum stop at N_max, and `mean` is the zero mode: every other statistic of the loss is blind beyond the Nyquist disk. This is not imposed by the mathematics: a spectrum could bin up to the corners, and wavelets could cover them, with more sample variance there (LF69, step 3).

With `var` in the loss, the scales beyond the disk are constrained by the variance alone. The synthesis, which mimics a maximum-entropy model, then leaves them close to Gaussian, and they can bias statistics that the loss does not hold, the PDF for instance.

So `var` leaves the synthesis loss, and stays in the statistics for users. Removing it cannot hurt: inside the disk, S2 and the spectrum already constrain the power, `mean` the zero mode, and the low-pass term of LF6 the lowest frequencies. The degrees of freedom of the model are then the disk, constrained by S2, the spectrum and `mean`. Beyond the disk the loss neither constrains nor moves the power: the start is band-limited and the running field is projected on the disk at every evaluation, so the power beyond the disk stays at zero. Statistics outside the loss, the PDF for instance, are compared with the pre-filtered target. On rectangular fields, the disk is the physical disk of LF64, an ellipse in mode numbers.

The maths and code choices that follow from this card are documented: its `M-` entry, and the docstrings of the synthesis functions (task 2).

## Done when

- `var` is not in the synthesis loss, and stays in the statistics;
- the spectral start has, inside the disk, the binned spectrum of the target within its statistical error, and no power beyond the disk; without a spectrum, its variance inside the disk is the target's to 1e-12 (float64; 16 × 16 and 32 × 32, `nbatch` 1 and 2, the batch scaled together); a given `init` and a resumed checkpoint are unchanged;
- with the cut in Fourier space, the field on which the loss is computed has no power beyond the disk at any evaluation, to rounding;
- the leak of task 3 is measured and reported;
- `make test-spec` passes; `SYNTHESIS_REFERENCE` bumped with CODEOWNER approval.
